# Acceptance: what counts as a proved claim of the paper

This directory holds the acceptance test of the formalisation. The report is
[`../ACCEPTANCE.md`](../ACCEPTANCE.md), generated by `tools/acceptance.py`.

## Three layers

1. **The statements** (`MVFBSDEJ/PaperSpec/`). Each claim of the manuscript, or each clause
   of one, is a closed proposition `MVFBSDEJ.PaperSpec.<label> : Prop`. It is written by
   reading the manuscript at its pinned revision, with the manuscript's hypotheses, quantifiers and
   complete conclusion: a constant said to depend only on some parameters is quantified before
   everything else, as a function of those parameters. It may use definitions of the
   implementation (the coefficients, the filtration, the stochastic integrals, the laws) once
   they are reviewed. It is not obtained by renaming what the implementation proves.
2. **The implementation** (the rest of `MVFBSDEJ/` and the dependency `LevyStochCalc`). It
   may use other representations, stronger intermediate hypotheses and different proofs. None of
   that counts towards acceptance until it is assembled into a proof of a statement of layer 1.
3. **The proofs** (`MVFBSDEJ/Acceptance/`). For a proved statement, a theorem
   `MVFBSDEJ.Acceptance.<label>` whose type is *exactly* the proposition of layer 1.
   Since the proposition is closed, the theorem takes no premise the manuscript does not state.
   A missing bridge between the implementation and the statement is a missing proof, not a
   caveat.

## The frozen inventory

`claims.json` lists every claim of the paper at the pinned revision (every numbered environment,
and the equations, prose claims and proof steps recorded by the claim inventory), each with a
stable identifier and the hash of its source text; `tools/acceptance.py` checks those hashes
against the manuscript. Each claim has a scope:

| Scope | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `formal` | a mathematical claim to be proved |
| `definition` | a definition or assumption: its Lean definitions are reviewed, nothing is proved |
| `part-of` | a clause of another claim (named in `scope_note`), covered there |
| `duplicate` | the same claim as another (named in `scope_note`) |
| `proof-step` | a step inside a printed proof; the proof of the enclosing result supersedes it |
| `expository`, `literature`, `empirical` | no mathematical claim of the paper to prove: exposition, a statement about cited work, a numerical output |

A scope decision taken by the automatic first pass is marked `scope_reviewed: false` until it is
checked by reading. A claim leaves the target only through a recorded scope decision; none is
dropped because it is hard.

A formal claim has one or more clauses. A clause names its proposition (`spec`, or `null` while it
is not written), its proof (`accept`) or refutation (`refute`), for a corrected statement the
clause it corrects (`corrects`), and optionally a `witness`: a theorem of the acceptance layer
stating that the proposition's hypotheses hold at an instance the manuscript intends (see
Reviews). The check verifies that the witness is a theorem over the three standard axioms; that
its statement matches the proposition's hypotheses is the review's judgement.

## Reviews

A proposition is *reviewed* for a claim when `review/A.md` records a review of it, with a marker
`<!-- spec-review: <name> <hash> by=<reviewer> source=<claim>@<digest> -->` at its current hash
whose `source=` digest for that claim is the claim's current `source_sha256`: the digest of the
claim's source passage, in the scheme of its pin, as it was read. A marker lists one `source=` for
each claim whose passage the reading covers; a change of the printed text therefore reopens the
review for that claim, and `check` names the claim whose passage changed since its reading. `by=`
names the reviewer, and the report shows it (`claude-author` marks a review by the session that
wrote the statement, which is not an independent review). The meaning of a proposition also
depends on the definitions it uses: every declaration of `MVFBSDEJ` or `LevyStochCalc` in its
definition closure needs a marker `<!-- def-review: <name> <hash> by=<reviewer> -->` in
`review/definitions.md` (Mathlib and the pinned upstream libraries are taken as foundations).
A definition named by a definition claim is read against that claim's passage, and its marker
records the passage digest in the same way. The hashes cover the types and the values of the
definitions with proofs erased, so any change of meaning, here or in a definition the
proposition depends on, reopens the review. `tools/acceptance.py hashes <name>` prints the
current hashes, with the current passage digest of every claim that uses the name; a reviewer
keeps only the `source=` entries of the passages actually read.

The hashes also record which instance elaboration chose, so a change of imports alone can move a
hash. A marker is carried to the new hash without a new reading only when its declaration's
source is unchanged and one of two checks reproduces the recorded hash: elaborating that source
again with the earlier imports, or re-resolving with the earlier imports each instance they supply
in the declaration, every replacement being definitionally equal to the instance it replaces.
Across a renaming of declarations, both checks hash each constant under its earlier name.

The markers recorded before the digests existed carry the digest of each claim's passage at the
manuscript revision pinned when the marker was added: `2608.23203v1`, `f598204` or `1f67672`.
A reading of a passage that has changed since then does not count until it is read again.

A review reads the manuscript passage and the proposition side by side and records, for each
hypothesis, quantifier and conclusion, where the proposition has it; for a proposition broader
than the printed claim, the instantiation that gives the printed claim; every disagreement about
the manuscript's meaning, with how it was resolved; and, for a proposition with hypotheses, a
theorem showing that they can be met by an instance the manuscript intends (a proposition whose
hypotheses cannot be met is proved vacuously, so without such a witness the review is not
complete).

## Status of a claim

* **accepted**: every clause has a reviewed proposition and a proof whose type is exactly that
  proposition, depending only on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, and every
  review is by a reviewer other than the author of the statements;
* **proved, author-reviewed**: as accepted, but some review is only by the author of the
  statements (`by=claude-author`), which does not check their meaning independently;
* **proved, review pending**: proved, but a proposition or a definition it depends on is not
  reviewed at its current hash;
* **refuted as printed**: an original clause has a proof of the negation of its proposition; the
  corrected statement is a separate clause naming it (`corrects`), proved or not, and is never
  substituted for the original. The status adds *corrected statement proved* when every refuted
  clause has a proved correction, and *other clauses open* when some other original clause is
  not proved: a refutation never hides an open clause;
* **open**: some clause has no proof, or no proposition yet;
* **unspecified**: no proposition written;
* **malformed**: the clauses are only corrections, with no original clause.

The check also validates the clause graph: clause names are unique within a claim, and every
`corrects` names an original clause of the same claim, never itself or another correction.

Nothing else promotes a claim: not a ledger tier, a declaration count, a clause table, or a
platform badge.

## Rules for the statements

* A statement changes only in a commit that says why, with its review updated in the same commit.
  A change made to fit what the implementation proves is not allowed: if the printed claim is
  false, it is refuted and a corrected statement is added beside it.
* A proposition may be broader than the printed claim only when the printed claim is a recorded
  instance of it.
* No module of `MVFBSDEJ/PaperSpec/` imports `MVFBSDEJ/Acceptance/` (checked).

## The final check

On a clean clone at the release commit: `lake build`, then `python3 tools/acceptance.py check` (no
cached results are reused: the probe elaborates every statement and proof afresh), then
`bash tools/comparator_check.sh <dir>`: comparator (leanprover/comparator at the toolchain's tag)
builds a challenge module holding each proved statement and the negation of each refuted one with a
`sorry` proof, exports it and `MVFBSDEJ.Acceptance` with lean4export, checks that each proof has
exactly the challenge's statement, every declaration the statement uses being the same in both
environments, and that it uses only the three standard axioms, and replays the exported proofs into
a fresh kernel. It rejects a proof whose type is another statement. A pass certifies that the listed
propositions have kernel-checked proofs over the three standard axioms and that the reviews are
current; whether each proposition says what the paper says is the reviews' judgement.

`check` is a development consistency check: open, unspecified and unreviewed claims are statuses,
not failures, so its PASS says nothing about the paper as a whole. The whole-paper check is

    python3 tools/acceptance.py release-gate --comparator-result <dir>/comparator_result.json

which fails, listing every reason, unless the manuscript sources are present and current, the
consistency check is clean, every scope decision of the paper is reviewed, every formal claim is
accepted (every clause proved, every review current and by a reviewer other than the author of
the statements), every definition claim is reviewed likewise, no printed clause is refuted (a
refuted clause has to be corrected in the released manuscript, not only beside it in the
inventory), and the comparator result certifies a successful kernel replay of every proof of the
paper in the current environment.

The comparator result is `comparator_result.json`, which `tools/comparator_check.sh` writes beside
the log (`acceptance.py comparator-bind`). It records the exit status of comparator, whether the
log reports the replay, the theorems of the configuration, the digests of the configuration and
the challenge module, and the digests of the environment (the toolchain; the Lean sources, the
inventory, the tools that generate the challenge and the package files of this repository, whose
manifest pins every git dependency by revision; the Lean sources and package files of
LevyStochCalc), taken before and after the run.
The gate recomputes the environment and rejects a result whose process failed, whose log has no
replay, whose environment changed during the run or differs from the current one, whose
configuration or challenge file is not the recorded one, or which omits a proof of the paper. A
log alone is not evidence: names identify declarations, not their content.

Trust boundary: a result written on a developer's machine binds the evidence to its inputs, so
stale evidence and evidence for another environment are rejected, but whoever can write the file
can forge it. A release has to take the evidence directory from a run it controls: the `certify` job
of `.github/workflows/ci.yml` runs `tools/comparator_check.sh` and the release gate on every push
to `main`, and publishes the directory as the run's `comparator-evidence` artifact.

`python3 tools/test_acceptance.py` holds regression tests of the status logic, the clause
validation and the gate.
