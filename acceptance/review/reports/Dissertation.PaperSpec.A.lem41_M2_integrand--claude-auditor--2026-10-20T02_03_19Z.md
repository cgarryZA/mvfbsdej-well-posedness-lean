(or only the measurability part) is broader and fine; the instantiation is the delta U of Lemma 4.1.

What a faithful statement contains. For delta U given by a P (x) cal E-measurable map
(omega, s, e) |-> delta U_s(omega)(e) in R^m (the representative of an H_N element) for the setting's
filtration:
 (a) the map (omega, s, e) |-> e^s |delta U_s(omega)(e)|^2 (Euclidean norm squared, real exponential
     of the time s) is P (x) cal E-measurable, the predictable sigma-field being that of the setting's
     filtration (not an arbitrary filtration unless the statement is uniform over filtrations, which is
     broader and harmless, nor a smaller/larger sigma-algebra such as the progressive or product one,
     which would be a different notion: "progressive" or "jointly measurable" would be weaker);
 (b) e^s |delta U_s(omega)(e)|^2 >= 0 for all (omega, s, e) (pointwise; a.e. would also be acceptable).
The time domain: s in [0,T] (or R_+ restricted to [0,T]); time is the real time variable.
If delta U is represented as an L^2(nu)-valued process, the statement must pass to a
P (x) cal E-measurable representative (fubiniiso) and the integrand is understood through it; the
measurability must be joint in (omega, s, e) with respect to P (x) cal E, not merely for each fixed e.

Vacuity: the hypothesis (delta U predictable, possibly square-integrable) is met e.g. by delta U = 0
or any deterministic measurable U in L^2(dt x nu); a witness should show the hypotheses satisfiable
in the setting.
