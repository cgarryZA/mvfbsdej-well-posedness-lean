Paper notion. Section 2, lines 222-229: "(F_t) is the usual augmentation of the one generated by W, N and
an independent initial sigma-field F_0"; the space "satisfies the usual conditions". So
F_t = usual augmentation (null sets; right-continuity) of
   G_t := F_0 v sigma(W_s : s <= t) v sigma(N(A) : A in B([0,t]) (x) E),
the N-part being generated by the counts of N on measurable subsets of [0,t] x E (equivalently of
(0,s] x B, s <= t, B in E, or of such sets of finite intensity).

What a faithful Lean definition must be.
- A Mathlib Filtration (time index [0,oo) or [0,T]) with F_t = augmentation of G_t as above, by the
  P-null sets of the driver's probability measure. As in unit 09, adding null sets alone suffices in this
  setting (right-continuity follows), and in Lean the null sets should be measurable ones (so F_t <= the
  ambient sigma-algebra).
- It must include all three generators: F_0, all d components of W up to time t, and N restricted to
  [0,t] x E. Missing F_0, or using N on all of R_+ x E (future information), or W values beyond t, would
  be wrong. Using N on [0,t) x E versus [0,t] x E is harmless after augmentation (N({t} x E) = 0 a.s.
  for each fixed t... but only when nu(E) < oo; for nu(E) = oo, N({t} x B) = 0 a.s. for each B of finite
  measure, and sigma(N on [0,t] x E) is generated by finite-measure pieces, so still harmless).
- It should be the filtration with respect to which the stochastic integrals of the propositions are
  defined and adaptedness/predictability are required.
