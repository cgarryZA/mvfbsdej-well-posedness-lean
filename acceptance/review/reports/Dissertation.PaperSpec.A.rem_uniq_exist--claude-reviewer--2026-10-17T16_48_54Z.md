# Reading: spec rem_uniq_exist (Remark 3.4, clause "the existence requires Assumption 2.2:
(2.3) and Assumptions 2.3-2.5 alone do not give a solution")

Paper sentence: "However, the existence requires Assumption 2.2." In context (Remark 3.4 follows
Theorem 3.3, uniqueness under Ass. 2.3-2.5 only; Theorem 3.5 gives existence under 2.2-2.5), the
mathematical content is that Assumption 2.2 cannot be removed from Theorem 3.5: the conclusion
"a solution in S exists" does not follow from the standing setting + Ass. 2.3-2.5 alone. The
only formal reading of "requires" is a counterexample (negation of the universal statement):

  NOT ( for every standing setting and every admissible coefficient system satisfying the
        standing measurability [(2.3) Borel measurability of h into L^2(nu), and joint Borel
        measurability of b, sigma, f, g] and Assumptions 2.3, 2.4, 2.5, a solution exists ),

i.e. EXISTS a standing setting and a system meeting all of these, with NO solution in S.

What a faithful formal statement must contain:
1. Setting: a genuine standing setting of Section 2 -- T > 0, n, m, d >= 1, a filtered
   probability space with usual conditions carrying a d-dim Brownian motion W and an independent
   Poisson random measure N with intensity dt x nu, nu sigma-finite on a countably generated
   (E, calE), filtration = augmentation generated by W, N and an independent F_0. If the
   statement is "for every setting there is a counterexample" (stronger than needed), the
   setting type must be inhabited (else vacuous); if "there exists a setting", the setting must
   be a real one (not degenerate, e.g. d >= 1 with a genuine BM if the counterexample uses the
   Brownian channel).
2. Coefficient system: deterministic b, sigma, h, f, g with the paper's signatures on
   [0,T] x H x P_2(H) (g on R^n x P_2(R^n)), jointly Borel; h Borel into L^2(nu;R^n) (2.3).
   Initial datum xi_0 F_0-measurable. (Keeping xi_0 in L^2 is not required by the bare negation,
   but a counterexample whose only defect is xi_0 not in L^2 is trivial; an informative one keeps
   xi_0 in L^2 and violates the coefficient origin-integrability part of 2.2.)
3. Hypotheses verified at the counterexample: Ass. 2.3 (diagonal Lipschitz with some L >= 0,
   a.e. t, all diagonal pairs in D_t; terminal (2.6) for all X_T, X'_T in L^2(F_T)), Ass. 2.4
   (G in R^{m x n} full rank), Ass. 2.5 (terminal expected G-monotonicity with alpha; interior
   (2.7) a.e. t on D_t with beta_1, beta_2; constants alpha, beta_1, beta_2 >= 0,
   alpha+beta_1 > 0, beta_1+beta_2 > 0, beta_1 > 0 if m < n, alpha, beta_2 > 0 if m > n). These
   must be the same predicates used by Theorem 3.5's statement.
4. Conclusion: there is no Theta in S = S^2 x S^2 x H^2_W x H^2_N solving (1.1) a.s. for every t
   (h at the predictable tuple, laws mu_t, mu_t^- of the tuple). Junk-value caution: the Lean
   notion of "solution" must be at least as permissive as the paper's at the counterexample
   (otherwise "no Lean solution" does not give "no paper solution"); in particular if the Lean
   solution predicate demands extra integrability of the coefficient processes, one must check
   that the paper's solution notion also fails there (e.g. a time singularity making the Ito or
   Lebesgue integral undefined even locally). Conversely, if stochastic/Bochner integrals return
   junk 0 on non-integrable integrands, non-existence has to hold despite that.
5. Necessarily the counterexample violates Ass. 2.2 (by Theorem 3.5). Constants: none to order.
