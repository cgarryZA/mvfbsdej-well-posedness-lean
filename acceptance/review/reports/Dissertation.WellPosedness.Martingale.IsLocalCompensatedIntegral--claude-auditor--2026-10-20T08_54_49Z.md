# Reading (locked before the Lean): IsLocalCompensatedIntegral

Paper's notion. The compensated Poisson integral int_0^t int_E H_s(e) Ñ(ds, de), Ñ = N - ds nu,
for P (x) E-measurable integrands (section 2, (2.8): every Poisson integral is read through a
P (x) E-measurable representative). In Lemma 4.1 and Lemma 4.3 it is taken for integrands that are
only locally square-integrable (int_0^T int_E |H|^2 nu(de) ds < inf a.s., e.g.
e^s <dY_{s-}, dU_s(e)>), and the result is called a local martingale (lines 139-147, 611-612);
for integrands in L^1(dP ds nu) (M^2, the fifth term) the paper instead uses the identity
int int H dÑ = int int H dN - int int H nu ds with (2.1)/(2.2). The standard construction
(Applebaum section 4.2; Ikeda-Watanabe II.3) for H with int int |H|^2 nu ds < inf a.s.: localise
by stopping times tau_k with E int_0^{tau_k} int |H|^2 nu ds < inf, take the L^2 compensated
integral (isometry, cadlag square-integrable martingale), and patch. The result is a cadlag local
martingale of (F_t) with jumps Delta M_s = H_s(e) at atoms (s, e) of N.

What a faithful Lean definition must be. A predicate on M : [0,T] x Omega -> R, an integrand
H : [0,T] x Omega x E -> R and the random measure N (with its compensator ds nu(de) relative to
the paper's filtration) such that:
 1. Identification: (F_t)-stopping times tau_k nondecreasing, exhausting [0, T] (tau_k >= T
    eventually a.s.), with H 1_{s <= tau_k} in the L^2 class (P (x) E-measurable, E int int |H|^2
    1_{s <= tau_k} nu ds < inf), and the stopped M_{t ^ tau_k} equal to the L^2 compensated
    integral of H 1_{[0, tau_k]} at time t (a.s. for each t, or indistinguishably), M_0 = 0.
    The L^2 compensated integral must be the genuine one: on simple predictable integrands it is
    the pathwise sum over the atoms of N minus the compensator, extended by the isometry.
 2. Uniqueness up to indistinguishability on [0, T], which needs cadlag paths of M on [0, T] in
    the predicate (or indistinguishability in the identification).
 3. Local martingale in the paper's filtration; note a stopped jump process: M_{t ^ tau_k}
    includes the jump at tau_k; localising by H 1_{[0, tau_k]} is consistent with that.
 4. The predicate must not depend on the choice of P (x) E-representative in a way that changes
    the integral (two representatives equal dP ds nu-a.e. give indistinguishable integrals); it
    must apply to integrands that are only locally (not globally) square-integrable, since that is
    the case of M^1 and of the (4.18) jump terms.
Not acceptable: a predicate satisfiable by M = 0 for any H, a pathwise "int H dN - int H nu ds"
definition used where the integrand is not dP ds nu-integrable (that may be undefined /
junk), or a filtration other than the paper's in which N is not a Poisson random measure.

supremum has zero-mean increments.

# Comparison: IsLocalCompensatedIntegral (Dissertation/WellPosedness/Martingale/LocalIntegral.lean)

Same structure as IsLocalBrownianIntegral, with the integrand phi : Omega -> R -> E -> R and the
L^2 compensated Poisson integral of LevyStochCalc (Poisson.Compensated.stochasticIntegral: the
cadlag adapted modification, for the right-continuous filtration, of the L^2 limit at every time of
the integrals of mark-step approximants; a square-integrable martingale of F^+ = F for the
right-continuous enlJoint; its difference isometry itoIsometry_diff_compensated shows it depends
only on the dP ds nu class of the integrand).

1. Identification: stopping times tau_k, monotone, T <= tau_k eventually a.s.; each cut-off
   phi 1_{s <= tau_k} jointly measurable, marked progressively measurable, of finite marked energy on
   every horizon; for every t in [0, T], a.s. on {t <= tau_k}, M_t = compensated L^2 integral of the
   cut-off at t. The cut-off 1_{s <= tau_k} includes the jump at s = tau_k, consistent with the
   stopped process M_{t ^ tau_k}. This is the localisation of the L^2 compensated integral of the
   reading; it applies to the locally (not globally) square-integrable integrands of M^1 and of the
   (4.18) jump terms. A non-locally-square-integrable phi makes the predicate unsatisfiable.
2. Uniqueness up to indistinguishability on [0, T]: as for the Brownian predicate, from the cadlag
   paths of M on [0, T] (every omega), locality of the compensated integral, and tau_k >= T
   eventually. Two representatives of the integrand equal dP ds nu-a.e. give integrals equal a.s.
   at each time (difference isometry), hence indistinguishable local integrals: the predicate does
   not depend on the choice of P (x) E representative.
3. Local martingale in the paper's filtration: follows as for the Brownian predicate (stopped
   process = L^2 compensated integral of the cut-off, a martingale of F^+ = F); the propositions use
   F = enlJoint, the paper's filtration, for which N is a Poisson random measure with compensator
   ds nu(de) on [0, inf) x E (IsPoissonFiltration; referenceIntensity = Leb|[0,inf) (x) nu).
4. Marked progressive (rather than marked predictable) cut-offs: the library's L^2 integral is
   defined on that class as an L^2 limit of predictable mark-step integrals and depends only on the
   L^2(dP ds nu) class, so every admissible integrand is a.e. equal to a predictable one and the
   integral is that of its predictable version, which for the paper's predictable integrands is the
   genuine compensated integral. (For a progressive but non-predictable integrand the predicate
   gives the integral of the predictable version, not a pathwise "int phi dN - int phi nu ds"; the
   paper never integrates such integrands.)
