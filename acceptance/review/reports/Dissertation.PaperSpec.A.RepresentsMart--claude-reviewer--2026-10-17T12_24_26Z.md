# Reading: "(Z, U) represents the martingale M" (def:Dissertation.PaperSpec.A.RepresentsMart)

Paper (§2, PRP paragraph; used in Example 5.2 and the proof of Lemma 4.2): every square-integrable
(F_t)-martingale M admits the representation
  M_t = M_0 + ∫_0^t Z_s dW_s + ∫_0^t ∫_E U_s(e) Ñ(ds, de)   for every t ∈ [0, T],
with M_0 F_0-measurable and (Z, U) a predictable square-integrable pair (Z ∈ H^2_W, U ∈ H^2_N),
unique up to null sets. In the Brownian reduction (Ex. 5.2) the Poisson term and U are absent.

A faithful Lean notion "(Z, U) represents M on [0,T]" (with M given): Z predictable
(R^d-valued, or per coordinate), U predictable marked (P⊗E-measurable), E∫_0^T |Z|^2 < ∞,
E∫_0^T ∫ |U|^2 dν dt < ∞, and for every t ∈ [0,T], almost surely, M_t = M_0 + ∫_0^t Z dW +
∫_0^t∫ U dÑ (stochastic integrals for the §2 filtration — or a parameter filtration for which
W is Brownian and N Poisson). "For every t" — a.s. per t, or a.s. simultaneously; equivalent for
càdlàg M given càdlàg versions of the integrals. M_0 itself is the value at 0 (its F_0
measurability is a property of M being adapted). The notion must not require more than the
paper's (Z, U) class (e.g. it may allow progressive measurability if the integrals coincide), and
must not allow non-square-integrable integrands. M scalar (or a coordinate of R^m).
