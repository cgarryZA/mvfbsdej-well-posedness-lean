Paper (§2, first paragraph): (F_t) is the usual augmentation of the filtration generated by W,
N and an independent initial sigma-field F_0; the basis satisfies the usual conditions.
So, given F_0 (a sub-sigma-algebra of the ambient one, independent of sigma(W, N)), the
filtration must be
   F_t = intersection over s > t of ( F_0 v F^W_s v F^N_s v Nulls ),
with F^W_s = sigma(W_r : r <= s), F^N_s = sigma(N(B) : B measurable subset of [0,s] x E) (the
natural filtration of the PRM), and Nulls the P-null sets of the (completed) ambient sigma-field
(the usual augmentation: completion by all P-null sets, then right-continuous regularisation;
the two orders give the same result for the usual augmentation). Times run over [0, T]
(t >= 0); values at negative times are irrelevant.

What a faithful Lean definition must be: a Filtration indexed by R (or [0,inf)) on Omega, built
from a sigma-algebra G0 <= ambient and a Levy driver D (W, N), equal at each t >= 0 to the
right-continuous, null-set-augmented version of G0 v F^W_t v F^N_t. Points to check:
 * F_0 is included at every time, not only at t = 0;
 * augmentation by null sets (of P, with the ambient sigma-algebra possibly not complete:
   then "null" should mean subsets of P-null measurable sets, or sets of the completion, and
   the filtration must still be a filtration of sub-sigma-algebras of the ambient space; with a
   non-complete ambient space one can only add measurable null sets — acceptable only if the
   statements do not need more);
 * right-continuity;
 * independence of F_0 from (W, N) is a hypothesis of the paper's setting (it may appear as an
   argument of the definition or of the statements, but the definition must not depend on a
   junk value when it fails).
Alternatively an equivalent formulation (a filtration that is shown to make W Brownian and N
Poisson and to carry the PRP) would be acceptable if it coincides with the paper's.
