Reading.
Paper notion: the compatibility of the filtration (F_t) with the Poisson random measure: (F_t)
is the usual augmentation of the filtration generated by W, N and the independent F_0, so N is
an (F_t)-Poisson random measure: 
 (i) adaptedness: for every t and every measurable B subset of E (at least those with finite
     nu-mass) and s <= t, N((0,s] x B) (more generally N(A) for measurable A subset of
     [0,t] x E) is F_t-measurable;
 (ii) independent increments with respect to the filtration: for every t, the family
     {N(A) : A measurable, A subset of (t, infinity) x E} (at least the increments
     N((t,u] x B), finitely many jointly) is independent of F_t.
These are what the paper's uses need: the compensated integral of a predictable integrand is an
(F_t)-martingale with the isometry; the cross terms in the isometry for several mark cells in the
same time interval need the joint (not only single) independence of the future increments from
F_t. A faithful Lean predicate IsPoissonFiltration F N: (i) and (ii) with joint independence of
the future increments (as a sigma-algebra, or for every finite family) from F_t. If only single
increments are required to be independent of F_t, it is weaker than the paper's setting; this
might still suffice for the uses if combined with the PRM's own independence of disjoint cells
(it does not in general: a single-increment condition does not give joint independence of
(Ntilde(A_1), Ntilde(A_2)) from F_t). The predicate is satisfiable (by the natural filtration of
N, and by the paper's filtration) - it must be, else propositions over it are vacuous. It is not
required to say that the filtration is generated by W, N, F_0 (that is a separate hypothesis).
