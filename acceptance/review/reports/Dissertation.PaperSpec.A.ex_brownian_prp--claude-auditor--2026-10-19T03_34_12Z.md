Paper claim. On the Brownian basis (d-dim BM W, F_0 independent of W, filtration (F_t) = usual
augmentation of the filtration generated by W and F_0, horizon T>0), every square-integrable
(F_t)-martingale M (R^m- or R-valued) can be written
  M_t = M_0 + int_0^t Z_s dW_s   for every t in [0,T] (a.s.),
with M_0 F_0-measurable and Z predictable, E int_0^T |Z_s|_F^2 ds < infinity (Z in H^2_W),
and Z is unique up to dt (x) dP-null sets. (This is the Brownian analogue of the sec. 2 PRP,
passed to the independent initial enlargement.)

A faithful formal statement must contain:
- Setting: arbitrary probability space carrying such W and F_0, filtration = augmentation of
  sigma(F_0, W_s : s <= t) (completion with P-null sets, right-continuous); W a BM for (F_t).
- Hypotheses: M a martingale for (F_t) on [0,T], E|M_t|^2 < infinity (square integrable); no
  extra hypothesis such as continuity or a specific form of M (a cadlag version may be assumed,
  harmless as every such martingale has one).
- Conclusion: existence of a predictable square-integrable Z with M_t = M_0 + (Ito int of Z)_t
  a.s. for each t in [0,T]; uniqueness of Z dt dP-a.e. (the sec. 2 PRP has uniqueness; the
  example says "the representation property is the Brownian one", which includes it). M_0
  F_0-measurable is automatic for an adapted M.
- Must use the Brownian (not Brownian-Poisson) filtration; if stated as "nu = 0 instance of
  the Brownian-Poisson PRP", the Poisson term must vanish genuinely.
- Hypotheses satisfiable (the existence of such a basis; any constant martingale).
