Reading for def:LevyStochCalc.Brownian.BrownianMotion (written before seeing any Lean).

Paper notion. Section 2: the filtered probability space (Omega, F, (F_t), P) "carrying a
d-dimensional Brownian motion W and an independent Poisson random measure N"; the filtration is
the usual augmentation of the one generated by W, N and an independent F_0. W drives the
d W-integrals int Z dW (Z in R^{m x d}) and int sigma dW (sigma in R^{n x d}). A (standard)
d-dimensional Brownian motion: W_0 = 0 a.s.; for 0 <= s < t, W_t - W_s ~ N(0, (t-s) I_d);
increments over disjoint intervals independent; a.s. continuous paths. Relative to the
filtration (F_t): W adapted and W_t - W_s independent of F_s (an (F_t)-Brownian motion), which
holds for the paper's filtration because W, N, F_0 are independent.

A faithful Lean structure "BrownianMotion" must:
- bundle a process W : time -> Omega -> R (one-dimensional) or R^d with exactly the standard BM
  properties: W_0 = 0 (a.s. or everywhere), Gaussian increments with variance t - s (covariance
  (t-s) I_d), independence of increments (of finitely many disjoint increments jointly, not only
  pairwise), and continuity of paths (a.s. or everywhere — "everywhere" is a harmless
  strengthening on a chosen version only if it is still satisfiable);
- if it is one-dimensional, the d-dimensional W of the paper must be represented elsewhere as d
  such motions that are jointly independent (that is then a matter for the statements using it);
- no extra hypothesis that excludes the paper's W (e.g. a specific Omega, or W being the
  canonical process), and no missing hypothesis that would admit non-Brownian processes;
- time set: [0, infinity) or [0,T] are both fine for a fixed horizon T.
Filtration-relative properties (adaptedness, independence from F_s) may be a separate predicate
(IsBrownianFiltration); then BrownianMotion itself need only be the law-level BM.
