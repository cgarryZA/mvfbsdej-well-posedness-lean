UNIT def:Dissertation.WellPosedness.Existence.dealerMarks
Paper notion (Example 5.5, "Marks and activity"): the mark space E = {-1,+1} x (0,1], e=(s,q) with
side s and size q, and the intensity nu(ds,dq) = c_s q^{-1-rho} dq, with constants c_s>0 (one per
side, c_{-1}, c_{+1}) and 0<rho<2; in the specialisation "the interaction is active" c_{+-1} =
(2-rho)/2. Consequences the paper uses: nu is sigma-finite, nu(E)=infinity (infinite activity),
int_E q^2 nu < infinity, so h_c(s,q)=s q and h_x(s,q)=q lie in L^2(nu), with ||h_c||=||h_x||=1 and
<h_c,h_x>=0 under c_{+-1}=(2-rho)/2.
What a faithful Lean definition must be: a measure on a type representing {-1,+1} x (0,1] (e.g.
a two-point type or Bool or a subtype of Z/R for the side, times (0,1] or R restricted to (0,1])
equal to sum over s of c_s * (Lebesgue on (0,1] with density q^{-1-rho}). The density must be the
genuine q^{-1-rho} on (0,1] (junk values of rpow at q<=0 must be excluded by the restriction to
(0,1]); the size part must be Lebesgue restricted to (0,1] (endpoints are null, so (0,1) or [0,1]
with the density only on q>0 are equivalent; but any positive mass at q=0 or outside (0,1] is
wrong). The constants c_s may be parameters (c_s>0) or fixed at (2-rho)/2; the exponent rho with
0<rho<2 a parameter (or fixed). The resulting measure must be sigma-finite and of infinite total
mass (if the Lean picked a parameter range where the measure is finite, it would not be the
paper's infinite-activity example).
