# Reading: sec4_horizon_not_contractive (paper line 886, §4 intro)

Paper text: "The continuation is in the coupling strength, not the horizon: shortening the horizon
does not make the frozen-coefficient map contractive, since the (z,u)-dependence of sigma,h and
the full-tuple law coupling enter the contraction constant with no T-factor."

The "frozen-coefficient map" is Phi_lambda of Lemma 4.2 (eq. frozenfwd/frozenbwd): given a trial
tuple Theta in S (the space S^2 x S^2 x H^2_W x H^2_N with norm E sup|X|^2 + E sup|Y|^2 +
E int |Z|^2 + |U|^2), Xbar solves the forward equation with coefficients frozen at
(Theta, Law(Theta)), (Ybar, Zbar, Ubar) solves the linear BSDE with terminal
lambda g(Xbar_T, Law(Xbar_T)) + q^g and driver frozen at Theta, via the PRP. "Shortening the
horizon" refers to the unscaled system lambda = 1 (at lambda <= lambda_* the map is a
contraction by Lemma 4.2); the claim is that one cannot obtain contractivity of Phi_1 by
choosing T small.

Faithful formal statement (clause as named: "on every horizon a system satisfying the
assumptions has a frozen map that does not decrease distances"):
- Setting: the standing Brownian-Poisson basis (filtered probability space with usual
  conditions, d-dim BM W, independent PRM with sigma-finite intensity nu on countably generated
  E, filtration generated by W, N, F_0). Ideally for every such basis (or at least for the
  standing one; an existential-over-basis reading is weaker but tolerable for an
  obstruction remark, to be noted).
- For every horizon T > 0:
- there exist coefficients (b, sigma, h, f, g), data xi_0 (and a coupling matrix G, constants
  L, alpha, beta_1, beta_2) satisfying Assumptions 2.2-2.5 (integrability at origin, diagonal
  Lipschitz, full rank G, terminal + interior jump-extended G-monotonicity with the constant
  constraints) on [0,T];
- such that the frozen-coefficient map Phi_1 (lambda = 1, forcing q = 0 or any fixed forcing)
  on S over [0,T] is NOT a contraction: there exist Theta^1 != Theta^2 in S with
  ||Phi_1(Theta^1) - Phi_1(Theta^2)||_S >= ||Theta^1 - Theta^2||_S
  (or, the clause's stronger form, ||Phi(Theta^1) - Phi(Theta^2)|| >= ||Theta^1 - Theta^2||
  for all pairs). Either form excludes a Lipschitz constant < 1; a form that only says
  Phi is not a contraction for some specific bad constant, or allows Theta^1 = Theta^2
  (trivial 0 >= 0) would be vacuous/wrong.
- Quantifier order: forall T, exists system. A form "exists T" would be weaker than claimed;
  a form "exists one system working for all T" would be stronger (and fine).
- The mechanism (no T-factor in the constant from z,u-dependence of sigma,h) is explanation;
  a witness like sigma = -z (or h = -u) satisfies monotonicity and gives
  E sup |dXbar|^2 >= E int |dZ|^2 = ||dTheta||^2 for dTheta = (0,0,dZ,0).
- Phi must be the real frozen map (forward SDE solution + PRP-based BSDE solution with laws of
  the trial tuple), not a surrogate; definitions of laws: Law(Theta_t), Law(Theta_t^-),
  the X_T law Law(Xbar_T).
- Non-vacuity: the existential is over systems, so the theorem must actually produce a system
  satisfying the assumptions; the statement must not be satisfiable by a degenerate S (e.g.
  if S could be a singleton the "exists Theta^1 != Theta^2" form would fail rather than be
  vacuous; the "for all pairs" form would be vacuous on a degenerate space).
