underline-lambda in (0,1], lambda in [underline-lambda, 1], all F_0-measurable initial data,

Ingredients the proof uses (so a faithful statement must make them available): the forward
difference equation (4.15) with delta X_0 = delta xi_0; Assumption 2.3 at diagonal inputs for
a.e. t together with the coupling bound eq:coupling W_2(mu^1_t, mu^2_t)^2 <= E|dTheta_t|^2;
BDG and Cauchy-Schwarz; Gronwall using finiteness of E sup|dX|^2 (from Theta^1-Theta^2 in S);
lambda <= 1 so lambda^2 factors are absorbed into C. The term P includes delta q^g and
delta q^f although the forward equation does not involve them (harmless, makes the bound
weaker than needed).

A statement with fewer hypotheses (e.g. without 2.4/2.5, any real lambda in [0,1], or with
the solutions only required to satisfy the forward identity) is broader and acceptable.
Narrower would be: requiring each Theta^k in S (instead of the Lemma 4.3 regularity),
requiring Assumption 2.2, letting C depend on lambda, underline-lambda, dimensions, nu, the
coefficients or the data, a restricted basis (e.g. finite nu only), or a different P.
Norms must be Euclidean / Frobenius / L^2(nu) (a different matrix norm changes the meaning of
Assumption 2.3 by dimension-dependent factors, which would conflict with dimension-free C).
