Paper notion: the law of N(A) for the Poisson random measure: Poisson with mean
lambda(A) = (dt x nu)(A) in [0, infinity]. A definition named poissonMeasureENN is presumably the
Poisson distribution as a probability measure on [0, infinity] (ENNReal), parameterized by a
mean lambda (in ENNReal or NNReal). Faithful: for finite lambda, the law
sum_{k >= 0} e^{-lambda} lambda^k / k! delta_k (the image of Mathlib's Poisson pmf under
N -> ENNReal), with lambda = 0 giving delta_0; for lambda = infinity (if the parameter admits it)
the Dirac mass delta_infinity, the standard convention for infinite-intensity sets. A junk value
for lambda = infinity, e.g. delta_0 or Poisson(0) through toNNReal infinity = 0, would be
unfaithful and dangerous: it would force N(A) = 0 a.s. for every infinite-intensity A, which is
impossible for a PRM with nu(E) = infinity (N((0,1] x E) >= N((0,1] x B_k) for finite-mass
B_k whose intensities tend to infinity), making every PRM with infinite-activity nu nonexistent
and all propositions over such PRMs vacuous - unless the PRM definition only applies this law to
finite-intensity sets. Must be a probability measure.
