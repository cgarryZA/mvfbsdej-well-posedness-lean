with q = q^1 - q^2 (so lambda delta g_T + q^g_T = delta Y_T) and delta f at (Theta^k_s, mu^k_s).
No constant appears; the inequality is uniform (it is in fact an equality, Ito's formula after
taking expectations, the jump sum contributing E int ||delta U||^2), so the quantifiers are
forall kappa > 0, forall t in [0,T], after the setting, coefficients and solutions. The quantity
2 E int e^{kappa s} <delta Y_s, lambda delta f_s + q^f_s> ds is a signed real, meaningful because
the integrand is integrable on [t,T] x Omega (delta Y in S^2, delta f in L^2 by Assumption 2.3,
q^f in L^2); the left side terms and the terminal term are finite for the same reasons. A faithful
formalisation must either assert the integrability of these integrands or use expressions that
coincide with the true expectations under the hypotheses; with real Bochner integrals, a junk
value 0 for a non-integrable term would change the claim, so it matters that the hypotheses force
integrability (or that the statement asserts it). The derivation uses Ito's formula, the
zero-mean property of the three martingale terms (needs delta Y in S^2, delta Z, delta U
square-integrable) and Assumption 2.3 only for integrability; Assumptions 2.4-2.5 are not used,
so a statement with fewer hypotheses is broader. Restricting kappa (e.g. to kappa large) or t
(e.g. to t = 0 only) would be narrower. A witness should exhibit an intended instance.
