Reading of spec ex_brownian_prp (Example 5.2: "Remove the Poisson channel from the stochastic
basis, so that the filtration is generated by W and F_0 and the representation property is
the Brownian one"), clause "the representation property at nu = 0 is the Brownian one".

Paper: the standing PRP (cited): every square-integrable (F_t)-martingale M satisfies M_t =
M_0 + int_0^t Z dW + int_0^t int_E U dNtilde for all t, F_0-measurable M_0, predictable
square-integrable (Z,U), unique up to null sets. Example 5.2 removes the Poisson channel; the
formalisation models removal as nu = 0 (then N = 0 a.s., Ntilde = 0, L^2(nu) = {0}, so the
filtration is the augmentation of sigma(W, F_0) and the U-coordinate is trivial). That modelling
is acceptable: at nu = 0 the basis IS the Brownian basis up to null sets.
Faithful statement: at nu = 0, the representation property of the basis is the Brownian one:
every square-integrable (F_t)-martingale M has M_t = M_0 + int_0^t Z_s dW_s for all t in
[0,T] a.s., with M_0 F_0-measurable and Z predictable with E int |Z|^2 < infinity, unique up to
dt (x) dP-null sets. Two acceptable forms:
 (a) unconditional: the Brownian PRP holds for the augmented filtration of (W, F_0) [a
     substantial theorem: Ito's representation with an independent initial enlargement];
 (b) relative to the standing BP-PRP (which the paper cites and treats as part of the
     setting): if the BP-PRP holds (as in the standing basis) and nu = 0, then every
     representation has vanishing Poisson part, so the BP-PRP is exactly the Brownian PRP
     (equivalence of the two statements at nu = 0, or BP-PRP => Brownian PRP).
 Form (b) is faithful only if the standing basis of the Lean carries the BP-PRP as part of its
 setting (as the paper does). A statement that only shows "the Poisson integral vanishes at
 nu = 0" without connecting to the representation of every martingale would be narrower.
