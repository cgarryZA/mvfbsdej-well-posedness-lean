Paper content. For t>0, F_t supports a uniform variable (a function of W^1_t), so by Kallenberg
Lemma 4.22 every coupling of two laws in P_2(H) is the joint law of a pair of F_t-measurable
inputs. Hence the diagonal hypotheses, Assumption 2.3 (Lipschitz: for a.e. t, all diagonal pairs,
a.s. bound |db|+|dsigma|_F+||dh||+|df| <= L(|dX|+|dY|+|dZ|_F+||dU||+W2(mu,mu')); and terminal:
for all X_T,X'_T in L2(F_T), |dg_T| <= L(|dX_T| + W2(rho,rho')) a.s.) and Assumption 2.5
(terminal E<dg_T,G dX_T> >= alpha E|G dX_T|^2; interior expected inequality (2.7) for a.e. t and
all diagonal pairs; constant constraints), are intrinsic: properties of the coefficients, not of
the standing basis. Since interior conditions are dt-a.e. and terminal ones at T>0, no richness of
F_0 is needed.

A faithful formal statement must contain:
- two arbitrary standing bases (any two filtered probability spaces carrying W and an
  independent N with the same T, n, m, d, (E, calE, nu), filtration the augmentation generated by
  W, N, F_0), and the same deterministic coefficients (b,sigma,h,f,g) with the same constants;
- conclusion: Assumption 2.3 holds on basis 1 iff on basis 2, and Assumption 2.5 holds on basis 1
  iff on basis 2 (with the same L, resp. G, alpha, beta1, beta2). An equivalent form: each is
  equivalent to a basis-free formulation over couplings of laws in P_2(H) (resp. P_2(R^n)) -
  that implies the two-basis statement and would be acceptable (possibly broader/equal).
- the time quantifier as printed: "a.e. t" for the interior conditions (the null set before the
  pairs), terminal at T>0; the basis-independence argument needs t>0 only, so a.e. t suffices.
- Watch: a statement that only covers one direction, only Assumption 2.3 or only 2.5, or that
  restricts bases (e.g. requires the second basis to be an extension of the first, or rich F_0)
  is narrower; one that fixes a basis and says nothing about others is mismatched. The bases must
  be genuine standing bases (W Brownian, N Poisson with intensity dt nu, independence).
